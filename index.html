What is Financial Literacy?

Financial literacy refers to the ability to understand, analyze, and manage personal financial resources effectively in daily life. It includes budgeting, saving, investing, and debt management.

In modern society, financial literacy is essential due to rising living costs, digital banking systems, and increased access to credit. Poor financial knowledge may lead to debt accumulation and long-term financial instability.

✔ Outcome: Improves financial decision-making and long-term stability.

Budgeting

Budgeting is the process of planning, organizing, and controlling the allocation of income to different expenses such as daily needs, wants, and savings. It ensures that individuals do not spend more than what they earn, while helping them prioritize essential financial commitments. Through consistent budgeting, individuals are able to monitor their spending habits, identify unnecessary expenses, and make more informed financial decisions. Over time, this practice improves financial discipline, reduces the risk of overspending, and supports long-term financial stability and goal setting.

✔ Outcome: Encourages disciplined spending and financial control.

50/30/20 Rule

50% Needs → Essential expenses
30% Wants → Lifestyle spending
20% Savings → Future financial security

Saving

Saving is the habit of consistently setting aside a portion of income for future use, emergencies, and planned financial goals. It acts as a financial safety net that protects individuals from unexpected situations such as medical expenses, job loss, or urgent repairs. Developing a strong saving habit also encourages better financial planning and reduces reliance on borrowing. In the long term, savings can be used for major life goals such as education, property purchase, or investment opportunities, contributing to greater financial security and independence.

✔ Outcome: Builds financial security and independence.

Saving Examples

RM5/day = RM150/month
RM10/day = RM300/month
Emergency fund = 3–6 months expenses

Debt Management

Debt refers to borrowed money that must be repaid over time, usually with added interest. While debt can be a useful financial tool when used for important purposes such as education, housing, or business investment, poor debt management can lead to financial pressure and long-term instability. Effective debt management involves borrowing responsibly, maintaining a healthy debt-to-income ratio, and ensuring timely repayments. Without proper control, excessive debt can accumulate quickly, leading to increased financial burden and reduced ability to save or invest for the future.

✔ Outcome: Maintains financial stability and reduces risk.

Debt Guidelines

Avoid unnecessary loans
Keep debt below 40% income
Pay on time consistently
